Privacy Policy – Dial Deck

Effective date: 14 October 2026 · Last updated: 13 November 2026 (version 5.1.1)

This Privacy Policy explains what the Android app Dial Deck (package name com.himavarshan.dialdeck, "the app") accesses on your device and how it is handled. The app is published by Himavarshan Reddy ("the developer", "I", "me"). You can contact me at himavarshanreddy@gmail.com.

Summary: Dial Deck is a Phone app and a home-screen widget. Everything it uses (your calls, call history and contacts) stays on your phone. The app has no internet permission, no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no account. It never sends your data to me or anyone else. The one exception is a test report that you can choose to email to me yourself, described in section 5.

1. What the app does

Dial Deck can be your phone's default Phone app. It then shows the screen for incoming and outgoing calls, lets you answer, decline, hold, mute and end calls, shows your recent calls (Recents), favourites and contacts, notifies you of missed calls, and can block numbers. Its home-screen widget shows your favourites and, while Dial Deck is your Phone app, your recent calls; tapping a name calls that person, or opens a chat in WhatsApp or a new message, depending on the mode you chose. Calls themselves are carried by your phone's own calling service and your mobile carrier.

2. Information the app accesses on your device

Permission / accessWhat it is used for
Call log (READ_CALL_LOG, WRITE_CALL_LOG)Only while Dial Deck is your default Phone app: showing Recents in the app and widget, missed calls and the calls with a contact. It changes your call history only when you delete calls or clear your history, and to mark missed calls as seen.
Contacts (READ_CONTACTS, WRITE_CONTACTS)Showing names, numbers and photos. It changes a contact only when you ask: starring or unstarring a favourite, or choosing a ringtone for that contact.
My infoYour own details at the top of Contacts come from your Contacts app: its "Me" entry, or the contact you add or pick as yourself ("Add my info" opens your Contacts app, which saves it). Dial Deck only shows them; a tap copies a number or email to your clipboard.
Phone (CALL_PHONE, READ_PHONE_STATE)Placing calls, and knowing when a call ends so Recents refresh. With two SIMs, it also reads which SIMs can make calls, and the SIM's own number where Android gives it, so you can choose a SIM and see which is which. Balance codes you dial (like *123#) are sent to your carrier by your phone's own network service, which shows the reply; Dial Deck keeps neither. No IMEI or other identifier of your phone is read, and nothing is sent anywhere.
Calls (as the default Phone app)Android hands Dial Deck your calls so it can show the call screen and act on your taps. Optional call screening checks incoming numbers against your contacts on the phone if you turn on blocking of unknown or private callers.
Notifications, full-screen alerts, wake lockShowing incoming, ongoing and missed calls and call-back reminders; showing the call screen over the lock screen; turning the screen off when the phone is at your ear.
Alarms & remindersSo a call-back reminder you set arrives at the exact time. If you don't allow it, reminders still come, up to a few minutes late.
Start at boot (RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED)Keeping the widget up to date after a restart. No data is accessed.

3. How the information is used and where it stays

All of the information above is read and used only on your phone, to show it to you and to do what you tap. Numbers you block are kept in Android's own blocked-numbers list. If you choose to message someone, Dial Deck hands their number to WhatsApp or your Messages app, whose own privacy policies then apply. "Reply with a message" on a ringing call is sent by your phone's own messaging service. If you tap Share contact (or Share number), Android's share sheet opens and Dial Deck hands that contact's name and number(s), as text, to the app you pick; nothing is shared unless you choose an app.

4. What the app stores on your device

Your settings (appearance, colour, hand, widget choices), the order of your favourites, the number you chose for each contact, the SIM you chose for a contact and the names you give your SIMs, which contact is you (for My info), your quick replies, any call-back reminders you set (the number, name and time), and a short diagnostic log of what the call screen and call engine did, in which every phone number is shortened to its last two digits. This stays in the app's private storage and is removed when you uninstall the app. The app does not use cloud backup.

5. Data collection and sharing

Dial Deck does not collect or share your personal data. It has no internet permission, so it cannot send anything by itself.

Test reports (only if you send one): Settings › Help › Send test report opens your own email app with a report addressed to me. It contains the diagnostic log described above, crash details, your phone model, Android version and the app version. Every phone number in it is shortened to its last two digits; it contains no names, no full numbers and no call history. Nothing is sent unless you press send in your email app. I use reports only to fix problems. Send feedback works the same way: it opens your email app, and only what you write is sent.

6. Data retention and deletion

Data on your phone stays until you delete it or uninstall the app. Test reports and feedback emails you send are kept only as long as needed to fix the problem; email me and I will delete them.

7. Your choices and controls

You can stop using Dial Deck as your Phone app at any time (Android Settings › Apps › Default apps › Phone app); it then stops reading your call log. You can withdraw any permission in Settings › Apps › Dial Deck › Permissions, or uninstall the app, which removes everything it stored.

8. Security

Your data stays in Android's protected storage for the app and in Android's own call log, contacts and blocked-numbers lists. Because the app never transmits data, there is no server or account that could be breached.

9. Children's privacy

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from anyone.

10. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access or delete personal data. Since the app collects nothing, there is no data held about you, except a test report or email you chose to send, which I will delete on request.
